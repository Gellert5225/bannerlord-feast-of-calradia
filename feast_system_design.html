Part the First

Design Vision

Bannerlord shipped without feasts, and stripped Warband's courtship system down to a charisma check. The result is a kingdom of nobles who have nothing to do during peace and no rituals through which their relationships are formed. This system restores the missing layer.

§ I Why Feasts

The complaint most commonly levelled against Bannerlord by long-time fans is that its world is mechanically impressive and emotionally inert. Players cannot name the rulers of factions they have served for years. NPCs feel interchangeable. Late-game tedium sets in by the time a player has founded their own kingdom, because peacetime offers nothing beyond waiting for the next war.

Feasts solve all three problems with a single system. They are recurring social events that gather named nobles in a single room, give them reasons to interact, and produce consequences that persist. A player who has hosted a royal feast and danced with the ruler's daughter remembers it. So does the ruler.

§ II Core Principles

Feasts are events, not encounters.
A feast is composed of seven phases occurring over an in-game day or two. It is not a cutscene. The player is present throughout and makes consequential choices in each phase.
Standing is the currency.
Who you can invite, where you sit, who will dance with you, whose private audience you can request — all are gated by the social standing system, a composite of clan tier, renown, position, cultural match, and bloodline.
The world keeps moving.
Feasts do not pause campaign time. Wars can begin, fiefs can be attacked, caravans can be ambushed during a feast. This creates real tension and makes peacetime feel earned rather than guaranteed.
Information is partial, never preview.
Players choose toasts, dances, and tournament entries based on labels and context, not by previewing the exact text or rolling outcomes in advance. This is the core of replay value: reading the room is the skill.
Failure tolerance is asymmetric.
The room as a whole is forgiving — a bad toast costs you a few relation points across attendees. Specific targets are harsh — picking the wrong style for the lady you are courting can cost you her favour for the entire feast and beyond.
Persistence over polish.
What happens at a feast is remembered. NPCs reference notable toasts, duels, and embarrassments months later in dialogue. The feast is not over when you leave the hall.
Part the Second

System Architecture

Before describing the seven phases of the feast itself, the surrounding architecture must be specified — what causes a feast to be scheduled, who may host one, how guests are invited, and what occurs in the days between invitation and arrival.

§ III Triggers

With only one to two feasts per kingdom per year, each must have a reason. Random feasts feel hollow; commemorative feasts have weight. The scheduler picks one trigger per kingdom approximately every six months, weighted by what has actually occurred:

  • Coronation — a new ruler ascends, by election or succession.
  • Victory feast — the kingdom wins a major war or captures a regional capital.
  • Royal wedding — any clan-tier-4-or-greater marriage.
  • Treaty / peace celebration — the end of a long war.
  • Heir's coming-of-age — a faction leader's child turns eighteen.
  • Player-triggered — a fief-holding player chooses to host one.

Quiet kingdoms may go a year without a feast. Eventful kingdoms may have two within a season. The scheduler maintains a per-kingdom cooldown to prevent feast spam.

§ IV Hosting

Only fief-holding lords may host. This is a deliberate restriction: it makes feasts aspirational, and gives landless or low-tier players a reason to attend before they can throw their own. Hosting requires:

  • Peacetime with at least one neighbouring kingdom (a fully-mobilised kingdom cannot feast).
  • Treasury cost scaling with guest tier: roughly 5,000 denars for a modest gathering, 50,000 or more for a royal affair.
  • Food stores drained from the host fief's granary; low granary causes price spikes or quality reductions.
  • Lead time of seven to fourteen days between invitations and the feast itself, during which guests travel.
  • Influence cost for non-ruler vassals hosting royal-tier feasts (presumption above one's station).

The host configures three things: guest list, scale (modest, grand, or royal — affecting cost and prestige), and an optional theme tied to the trigger event. The hall scene used is the existing vanilla lord hall of the host fief; capacity scales with fief size:

FiefMax GuestsNotes
Castle (small)8Modest only
Castle (large) / Town keep20Modest or grand
Capital city30+Any scale, including royal

Each invited lord brings one to three family members within the cap. This makes capital cities meaningfully better venues — a tangible reason to want one.

§ V Invitations & RSVPs

This is where the social mechanics of the system earn their keep. When invitations go out, each invited clan independently decides whether to attend. The decision is driven by:

  • Relation with host (primary factor)
  • Distance from host fief
  • Current war or siege engagements
  • Presence of rivals on the guest list
  • Clan personality (some lords are reclusive, some are social)

RSVPs arrive as map notifications over two to three days, giving the player time to react. Lord Y declined — should I bribe him? Lady Z accepted, but so did her rival — drama is brewing. Snubs work both ways: failing to invite an allied clan is a relation hit, while failing to invite a rival is fine and expected.

Enemy kingdoms can never attend; the war state precludes it. During peace, cross-kingdom invitations are possible and prestigious. Inviting a neutral kingdom's leader to your feast is a diplomatic statement.

§ VI Travel & Arrival

Guests travel as small parties on the campaign map heading toward the host fief. This creates emergent stakes:

  • Bandits or hostile clans may ambush travelling guests. A guest dying en route is a story moment.
  • Late guests miss key activities; very late ones miss the feast entirely.
  • The player may choose to escort important guests, gaining significant relation.

When guests arrive at the fief, they remain there until the feast ends. The town or castle scene is repopulated with their notable characters walking around — already good ambience before the feast itself begins.

Part the Third

The Seven Phases

The feast unfolds across seven phases over an in-game day or two, each with its own activities, dialogue depth, and consequences. The player advances between phases via the host menu (when hosting) or follows the host's schedule (when attending). Each phase passes state to the next; what happened in Phase 2 affects what is possible in Phase 5.

The phases share an architectural pattern: an interaction menu, gated dialogue categories, capped relation deltas per phase, and explicit state passed forward to subsequent phases. Once Phase 1's structure is implemented, the others reuse the same scaffolding with their own twist.

I. Arrival & Mingling

Guests arrive. Greetings are exchanged. The room takes shape.

Setting

The player spawns at the hall entrance as host, or near the door as guest. All RSVP'd attendees who have already arrived are present, walking and standing in the hall. Late guests trickle in throughout the phase. Host's family members are present. Servants move with platters; a hired minstrel may play in the corner.

Player interactions

If hosting:

  • Greet arriving guests — auto-prompted dialogue when each guest enters. Mandatory; skipping causes a relation hit. Brief exchange (three to four lines), small relation gain.
  • Move freely between guests; click any to initiate dialogue.
  • Open host menu to advance to Phase 2, check guest list, view RSVPs, or dismiss the feast early (with consequences).

If attending:

  • Greet host — first action, auto-prompted, +2 relation with host.
  • Greet host's family — optional, +1 each.
  • Move freely between guests.
  • Leave early — possible but a heavy relation hit with host.
Dialogue categories
  1. Pleasantries. Available with any guest, always. Light relation gain (+1), no risk. Cap of one to two pleasantry exchanges per guest per phase.
  2. Topical conversation. Gated by mutual interests (each lord has two to three interest tags: war, trade, hunting, scholarship, religion, intrigue). Shared interest unlocks deeper topics: +2 relation, possible quest hook or rumour. Clashing interest applied wrongly: −1 relation. This is also where introduction quests fire — the intermediary you have been befriending finally walks you over to your courtship target.
  3. Strategic conversation. Gated by clan tier and standing. Diplomacy, recruitment, conspiracy. Mostly stubbed in v1 to seed v2 content.
Relation math

+1 for pleasantries, +2 for topical match, −1 for topical clash. Capped at +5 / −3 with any single guest in this phase. The phase is not meant to be a relation farm — that is what the entire feast is for, distributed across phases.

State produced
  • List of guests greeted
  • List of guests with conversation completed (caps later relation gain)
  • Tension pairs flagged (for Phase 6)
  • Active romance partners flagged (for Phase 5)
  • Quest hooks accepted or refused
Scope

v1 All of the above. v2 Strategic conversation depth (recruitment dialogues, conspiracy stubs become real branching). v3 Per-culture greeting flavour.

II. Toasts & Speeches

A sequence of toasts in order of precedence. Composition is style × subject. Reading the room is everything.

Composition system

Each toast is composed of one subject and one style, picked from menus without preview of the actual text.

Subjects (v1): the host · the occasion · fallen kin / the departed · the realm or kingdom · a specific person present · the future.

Styles (v1): solemn · boastful · witty · humble · romantic.

Six subjects × five styles = thirty cells. Each cell has two to three written variants, for a total of seventy-five to ninety unique toast strings. These are short — three to five sentences each — and constitute a substantial but tractable v1 content task.

What makes a toast land

Three layered factors determine reception:

  1. Subject appropriateness. Each feast trigger has an appropriateness map. Toasting fallen kin at a wedding is jarring unless culturally appropriate. Toasting the future at a funeral feast is offensive.
  2. Style appropriateness. Each subject pairs naturally with certain styles. Fallen kin demands solemn or humble; witty is offensive. Heirs and the future welcome romantic or boastful; solemn is heavy-handed.
  3. Personal preferences of key listeners. Every notable NPC has hidden style preferences. A lady who prefers solemn courtly style remembers a solemn toast favourably even if the room was boastful. This is the same axis system used in courtship (when the marriage system arrives).
Skill check on delivery

Composition determines content; Charm determines delivery. Even mediocre composition lands acceptably with high Charm. Even great composition can flop with low Charm.

Player UI
Step 1: What will you toast?
  > The host's hospitality
  > Our victory at [battle name]
  > Those who fell at [battle name]
  > The kingdom of [name]
  > [specific guest's name]
  > Whatever comes next for our houses

Step 2: How will you say it?
  > With solemn weight     [Solemn]
  > With proud fire        [Boastful]
  > With clever humour     [Witty]
  > With humble grace      [Humble]
  > With poetic flourish   [Romantic]

The labels give enough information to make a tactical choice. The exact words are the reveal.

Listening to others' toasts
  • Raise glass and drink — default, polite, neutral.
  • Cheer loudly — boosts speaker's renown gain, +1 relation, mild risk if the toast was bad (you look like a sycophant).
  • Stay silent / refuse to drink — only available if the toast genuinely insulted you; major relation hit with speaker but a statement.
Failure model (asymmetric)
SeverityEffect
Room-wide bad−1 to −2 relation with all attendees, −5 to −15 renown. Recoverable.
Targeted listener bad−5 to −15 with the specific NPC, "memorable embarrassment" flag for ~1 in-game year. Not recoverable this feast.
Critical failure−20 renown, −5 with all attendees, −15 with host, NPC dialogue references for months. Caps near 1% probability.
Scope

v1 Full grid, all reactions, listening interactions, ruler-present modifier, Charm checks. v2 Drunken NPC toasts, mid-feast initiative toasts, full memorable callbacks, cultural background modifiers. v3 Per-culture toast variants, named-toast callback dialogue.

III. Feasting

Seated at the tables. The longest phase. Where Warband-style hall conversations live.

Seating

Before Phase 3 begins, the host arranges seating. For player-hosts, this is a real decision with downstream effects via a drag-and-drop UI showing the high table and side tables. Constraints surface live:

  • Place of honour (right of host) — major prestige to whoever sits there. Snubs everyone else if not the highest-ranking guest.
  • Tension flags — seating two enemies adjacent triggers a UI warning; confrontation probability spikes.
  • Romance flags — seating you next to a courtship target advances romance, but is also a public statement.
  • Cultural mixing — small relation deltas based on whether cultures clash or harmonise.

For player-attendees, the host's NPC seating logic places you according to standing. Below a threshold, you are not seated at all — you stand and eat with the household, a major status signal.

Seated dialogue

Each seat has one to three dialogue partners (neighbours, across the table). Walking to far tables is allowed but socially costly with assigned neighbours.

  1. Personal stories. The partner shares something about their past. Player picks response style (sympathetic / curious / dismissive / one-upping). Right response: +3 to +5. Wrong: relation hit.
  2. Opinions on others. Gossip about guests or absent lords. Agree, disagree, or deflect. Intelligence-gathering as mechanic — you learn the social map of the kingdom.
  3. Confessions and secrets. Gated by relation 50+ and trust. A drunk lord may reveal grievances, debts, feuds. Quest hooks. Stubbed in v1.
  4. Romance dialogue. If seated next to a courtship target, deeper romance unlocks here. Poetry recitation fires here when the marriage system arrives.
  5. Banter. Light, low-stakes. Useful with low-relation guests.
Drinking games

Opt-in minigame at the table. Wager (denars or pledge), opposed roll on Vigor + Endurance with random variance, three to five rounds, fold or push.

OutcomeEffect
WinCollect wager, small renown, +1 relation with opponent, mild buff with watching guests.
Lose gracefully (folded early)Pay wager. Neutral.
Lose badly (pushed and lost)Pay wager, renown loss, "drunken fool" flag for the rest of the feast.

Hard cap of two contests per feast. Proposing to a teetotaller is a relation hit. Refusing a contest framed as a challenge to honour is a relation hit. Unavailable during somber feast types.

Scope

v1 Seating UI, all five dialogue categories (confessions stubbed), drinking games full, tension carry-forward. v2 Full confessions/secrets, intelligence-gathering UI, quest hooks from dialogue, "as I told you at the feast" callbacks. v3 Pledged-favour drinking wagers, table-hopping consequences modelling.

IV. Entertainment

An optional tournament. Public stage for reputation. The political class is the audience.

Why tournaments at feasts

Two reasons earn this phase its scope: it reuses Bannerlord's existing arena code (tournaments are not built from scratch), and the audience is the political class. Winning a regular tournament gets you renown. Winning a feast tournament gets you renown plus relation gains with every attending lord, plus eligibility flags for romance, recruitment, and patronage.

Format

Host picks one of two formats in v1: melee (default for most feasts) or archery contest (Battanian default). Royal feasts may include both, with separate winners and separate champion's privileges.

Archery contest

Five shots per competitor. Bullseye = 10 points · inner = 7 · mid = 5 · outer = 3 · hit = 1 · miss = 0. Two-round format: qualifier (everyone shoots, top 4 advance) and final (top 4 shoot, highest score wins). Sudden-death single-shot tiebreakers. Total time roughly five in-game minutes — much faster than a full melee bracket.

Player participation
  1. Compete — enter the bracket. Standard arena combat (melee) or aimed shots (archery).
  2. Watch — spectate. Dialogue with other spectators. Light betting available.
  3. Decline visibly — sit it out. Mild relation hit with martial-personality guests at melee tournaments; archery is socially neutral to decline outside Battanian feasts.
  4. Withdraw mid-tournament — yield. Costs renown but avoids worse outcomes.
Outcomes
Player wins
Major renown, +5 with host, +2 with all attendees, eligibility flag with all attending clans, champion's privilege (present the prize to a chosen attendee for +10 with them and their clan), "Champion of [feast name]" tag for ~6 months.
Player loses
Small renown loss if eliminated early; small renown gain if reached semifinals. +1 relation with the lord who defeated you. No public consequence.
Player declines
Mild martial-personality relation hits at melee; nothing at archery (outside Battania).
NPC wins
Player can congratulate them in Phase 5 for relation gain. A rival winning is a flag — they may use champion's privilege to honour your courtship target, escalating Phase 6 confrontation odds.
Scope

v1 Optional toggle, melee and archery, full participation choices, all outcomes including champion's privilege, ruler-watches convention. v2 Joust format, mixed format for royal feasts, ruler-competes events, full feud-pair bracket interactions. v3 Per-culture tournament flavour, spectator betting depth.

V. Dancing & Private Conversation

The room loosens. Tables clear. Conversations become personal. The intimate phase.

Setting

Tables moved aside, central floor opened, smaller knots of guests around the periphery, some retiring to alcoves and balconies for private talks. Lighting dims (script). Background music continuous (existing tavern minstrel audio repurposed). The vanilla lord hall already has alcoves and side spaces usable as private-conversation zones without authoring new geometry.

Dancing — abstracted

Real dance animations are out of scope. Stylised abstraction instead:

  • Player approaches an attendee: "May I have this dance?"
  • They accept or decline (relation, romance status, prior promise).
  • Brief fade-to-text scene with single illustration.
  • During the dance, a short three-to-four-line dialogue plays.
  • Player picks a tone (light banter / earnest / bold).
  • Tone matched against partner's preferences (same axes as toasts).
  • Outcome: +2 to +6 relation, romance progress if applicable, possible small renown.

Limits: one dance per partner per feast. Two to three dances total per phase. Dancing with a married NPC's spouse without tacit approval flags jealousy.

Private conversations

Side rooms and alcoves enable longer, deeper exchanges than Phase 3's seated dialogue.

  1. Full romance scenes — confession, exchange of tokens, plans for future meetings. Romance climax window: with sufficient prior progress, courtship can formally advance to betrothal here.
  2. Conspiracy and recruitment — disaffected vassals propose defection. Stubbed in v1.
  3. Quest hand-offs — friendly lord asks a personal favour in private.
  4. Apologies and reconciliations — Charm-driven repair attempts with low-relation NPCs.
  5. Player-side confessions — admitting weakness, requesting patronage.
The romance climax window

This is the seam where feasts hand off to the marriage system (built later). With sufficient prior relation and romance progress, the player may formally declare intentions in private conversation. If accepted, a follow-up flag is set: the player must approach her guardian within thirty in-game days for the betrothal to hold.

Slot system

The player has three private-conversation slots in Phase 5. Once spent, mingling continues but no further private exchanges may be initiated. NPCs who seek out the player do not consume slots — those are free events. The player must choose: romance target, mysterious vassal, old friend with quest, rival to reconcile?

Scope

v1 Stylised dance with tone-matching, full private system, romance hooks (stubbed for marriage), apology/reconciliation, quest hand-offs, conspiracy stubs. v2 Multiple dance types with different tone-preferences, full conspiracy/recruitment, jealousy event modelling. v3 Per-culture dance flavour, expanded private locations.

VI. Confrontations

The hour is late. Wine has flowed. What was suppressed earlier surfaces now.

Tension score

An internal score accumulates across the feast and resolves here:

  • Pre-existing rivalry (relation < −30): +1 base per pair
  • Adjacent seating of enemies in Phase 3: +2
  • Bad toast targeting someone present: +1
  • Tournament: losing badly to a rival: +1
  • Champion honoured your courtship target: +2
  • Dancing with someone's spouse: +2
  • Drunken-fool flag set: tension contributions doubled
  • Drinking-game humiliation: +1
  • Public refusal to drink to a toast: +2
Probability formula
P(confrontation) = min(0.85,
                       tension_score × 0.15
                     + base_personality_modifier)

Tension 4 ≈ 60% probability. Tension 1 ≈ 15%. Hot-headed lords skew higher; self-controlled lords lower. At most one to two confrontations per feast, picking the highest-probability rolls; lower pairs receive a "tension simmered" flag instead.

Tension visibility (the (c) hybrid)

Throughout the feast, the player sees tension flags on NPC portraits — they know who is at risk and the contributing factors. They do not see the final probability roll. They know the dice exist; they don't see them land.

Pre-confrontation intervention

An opening interval of about five in-game minutes opens Phase 6. If the player noticed tension earlier, they may:

  1. Mediate — Charm-based defuse attempt. Success: tension drops. Failure: tension stays, mediator may catch heat.
  2. Separate — quietly guide one party away. Reduces tension by 1, costs influence or a small relation hit.
  3. Provoke — actively push toward confrontation. Risky and implicates the player.

Players who weren't paying attention have no opening interval. Engagement is rewarded.

Confrontation types
Type A — Verbal exchange (~50%)

Two lords trade insults. Player options if involved: take a side · mediate (Charm check) · mock both · stay out. Resolves on its own without intervention: both parties drop −3 relation, no further consequences.

Type B — Public challenge to duel (~30%)

The argument escalates. By feast etiquette, duels are not fought at the feast — they are scheduled for dawn outside the walls. Player options: accept (refusing is a major honour hit) · issue · withdraw (high Charm + consent) · champion (third party fights in stead). The duel itself reuses existing Bannerlord duel mechanics. Death of a clan tier 4+ noble at your feast is a major political event — −10 relation with all attendees as host, possible succession crisis if a ruler or heir.

Type C — Brawl (~15%)

Tempers snap. The hall erupts. Host options: Charm + Leadership check to restore order (success: order, both ejected, −5 with both; failure: brawl spreads, feast ends in disgrace, "the disastrous feast at [fief]" tag for years). Brawls almost always end the feast immediately, skipping Phase 7's normal flow.

Type D — Public revelation (~5%, dramatic)

Rare special-case requiring high tension and a flagged secret on one party. Public outing. Massive long-term political consequences. Stubbed in v1 as flavour text only; full branching is v2/v3 work pending the conspiracy/secrets system.

Host authority

The host carries social weight as the room's authority figure:

  • Demand silence — Leadership check, ends Type A immediately, reduces escalation odds for B.
  • Eject a party — major relation hit with ejected party, but ends the confrontation.
  • Take the brawl outside — for Type C, redirects chaos out of the hall, contains damage.
Scope

v1 Tension system, visible flags, intervention window, Types A and B in full, Type C with simplified resolution, Type D as flavour stub, host authority, duel scheduling using existing mechanics. v2 Full Type C brawl spreading, Type D revelation branching, conspiracy/secrets integration, cultural confrontation norms, ruler-involved confrontations. v3 Long-term reputation tracking affecting future feast invitations, kingdom-wide political fallout.

VII. Conclusion

A reflective close. The host's last word multiplies what came before.

Setting

The hour is very late. Most guests are tired but still present. Servants clear the last of the food. The minstrel has stopped or plays only soft, slow music. The mood has shifted from Phase 5/6's looseness to something reflective.

The closing toast

Reuses Phase 2's composition engine with a modified subject list:

  1. Gratitude to guests — the safe pick.
  2. Reflection on the night — naming a memorable moment (requires the system to surface Phase 2-6 events).
  3. The road ahead — looking forward.
  4. A specific person honoured — risky; singling out at the end excludes everyone else from the closing focus.
  5. Lament or remembrance — somber close. Appropriate at funeral feasts; jarring at celebratory ones.

Style options unchanged from Phase 2, but boastful is flagged risky — late-night boasting reads as drunken arrogance.

Multiplier on outcomes
Closing toast qualityEffect on accumulated outcomes
Good+20% positive amplification. Guests carry this feeling home as their dominant memory.
MediocreNeutral. Outcomes resolve as accumulated.
Bad−20% on positives, +20% on negatives. The last impression carries.
Critical failureLarge renown loss, "ended his own feast badly" tag. ~1% probability.
The outcomes screen

Sections in suggested order:

  1. Header — feast name, host, scale, trigger.
  2. Relations changed — per-attendee, sorted by magnitude, brief reason for each delta.
  3. Renown and influence — total with breakdown.
  4. Notable events — duels challenged, tournament champions, dances danced, secrets confessed.
  5. Outstanding obligations — quests accepted, duels scheduled, romance progress flags, return invitations owed.
  6. Cost summary (host only) — denars, food. Was the feast worth it?
Long-term consequences
  • Memorable feast tag — exceptionally good or bad feasts are remembered. NPCs reference them in dialogue for 6-12 months.
  • Standing changes — successful royal feasts permanently boost host standing; disasters do the inverse.
  • Romance flags — courtship advanced here continues progressing on the campaign map.
  • Conspiracy seeds — anything started in Phase 5 carries forward.
  • Duels scheduled — fire as a separate event the next dawn.
  • Quests accepted — appear in the player's quest log.
Scope

v1 Closing toast (5 subjects × 5 styles), outcomes multiplier, full outcomes screen, persistent flags, departure handling, war-interrupt handling. v2 Travel ambushes for departing guests, "recently hosted" fief status, named-feast callbacks. v3 Cultural variants on closing-toast conventions.

Part the Fourth

Cross-Cutting Systems

Several systems thread through every phase and must be designed once, used everywhere. They are described here so they need not be re-specified within each phase.

§ VII Social Standing

Standing gates access to feasts (invitations), seating placement within them, and which dialogue options are available. It is composed of:

ComponentSourceWeight
Clan tiervanilla 0–6Primary — sets the bracket
RenownvanillaRefines within the bracket
Positionlandless / vassal / fief-holder / rulerModifier
Cultural matchsame / different culture from hostModifier on dialogue and seating
Bloodlinechargen backstoryFloor — opens doors above current tier

Rolled into four standing tiers:

  1. Commoner — no clan or tier 0. Cannot attend feasts at all (turned away by retainers).
  2. Aspirant — tier 1, low renown. May attend modest feasts of minor lords. Ladies require formal introduction.
  3. Established — tier 2–3, decent renown. Most feasts accessible. Romance dialogue gated by venue type, not gender.
  4. Magnate — tier 4+. Doors open everywhere, including ruler audiences.

Introduction chains are the escape valve: every higher-tier lord has intermediaries (vassals, kin, retainers) who can introduce a lower-standing player after sufficient befriending. This turns the social map into a navigation puzzle.

Patronage — a higher-tier lord may formally sponsor the player after exceptional service, granting a temporary standing boost in their sphere. This gives landless players a meaningful progression arc.

Overreach consequences are public and graduated: first offence is a quiet refusal; persistence brings mockery and renown loss; egregious cases (a Commoner demanding audience with a Magnate) trigger humiliation events.

§ VIII NPC Preference Profiles

Every notable NPC has hidden style preferences across the same five-axis system used for toasts, dance tones, and (later) courtship poetry. The axes are:

AxisWhat appeals to a lord/lady favouring this style
SolemnGravity, ceremony, ancestral weight, religious formality.
BoastfulMartial pride, glory, prowess, public recognition.
WittyCleverness, irreverence, lightness, sharp tongue.
HumbleModesty, deference, self-effacement, quiet virtue.
RomanticPoetic flourish, emotional heightening, courtly ardour.

Each NPC has a primary preference (style they strongly favour) and an aversion (style that actively offends them). Other styles are neutral. This is a 5×5 matrix per NPC, but with most cells neutral, it can be stored compactly: two enum values plus a strength weight.

Preferences are revealed indirectly through dialogue with siblings, handmaidens, retainers, and friends — never directly displayed in the NPC's encyclopedia entry. The player must invest in social legwork to learn them. A lord's preferences may be hinted by his personality traits in vanilla (Honor → Solemn affinity; Mercy → Humble; Calculating → Witty; Valour → Boastful).

§ IX Tension Score

Defined fully under Phase 6. Carried as a per-pair integer accumulating from sources distributed across earlier phases. Visible to the player as flags on NPC portraits; hidden in its final probability roll.

§ X Memorable-Feast Callbacks

The system that allows NPCs to reference past feasts in future dialogue. Mechanically:

  • Feasts that produce notable events (exceptional toasts, scheduled duels, brawls, public revelations, tournament championships, romance climaxes) write to a persistent feast log per save.
  • Each entry has a tag, participants, location, severity, and an expiry (typically 6-12 in-game months for routine entries; permanent for catastrophes).
  • NPC dialogue trees gain conditional lines that fire when the player and an NPC have a shared feast log entry. "I remember your toast at Pravend — it stays with me." · "The night Lord X was challenged at your hall is still spoken of."
  • Catastrophes ("the disastrous feast at [fief]") affect future RSVP probability — lords are less willing to attend a host with a recent disaster on his record.

This is the system that makes feasts matter beyond the night they occur. Without it, the feast is a forgotten cutscene. With it, the feast is a thread in the campaign's emergent narrative.

Part the Fifth

Implementation Roadmap

The design above is approximately three to five months of solo development at full-time pace, or six to nine months at consistent part-time. This section breaks the work into ordered phases, identifies risks honestly, and proposes a minimum playable build that gets you into testing as fast as possible.

§ XI Calibration on Confidence

Honesty Check

I am confident on Bannerlord's general modding architecture (C# with Harmony, CampaignBehaviorBase, MissionBehavior, the dialogue system using XML and conditions/consequences, save data via SyncData). I am not confident on exact namespace paths, specific event names, internals of the romance and tournament systems, or the precise way to hook into seated dialogue mid-mission. Where I name a class or event below, treat it as a hypothesis to verify, not a citation. The reliable path forward is to decompile TaleWorlds.CampaignSystem.dll with dnSpy or ILSpy, and reference the source of existing mods that touch related systems.

Mods worth reading source for, before writing your own:

  • Diplomacy — reference for kingdom-level events, alliance/peace mechanics, and persistent kingdom data.
  • Family Tree — reference for hero relationship data and persistent flags on heroes.
  • Improved Garrisons — reference for clean CampaignBehaviorBase patterns and MCM integration.
  • Bannerlord Tweaks / BannerLib — reference for Harmony patch patterns and common pitfalls.
  • Adonnay's Troop Changer or any custom-mission mod — reference for spawning agents into existing scenes.

§ XII Technical Stack

Language
C# (.NET) targeting whatever framework version the current Bannerlord build expects. Match the version of an actively-maintained mod's .csproj.
Harmony
For patches against game internals. Standard pattern for modding Bannerlord.
MCM
Mod Configuration Menu (community library) for in-game settings. Essential for tuning numbers without recompiling.
Gauntlet UI
Bannerlord's own UI framework, used for any custom panels (toast composition, seating UI, outcomes screen). Nontrivial; budget time to learn it from existing mods.
Save data
Custom CampaignBehaviorBase subclasses with SyncData implementations. Persistent feast log, tension scores, memorable-feast tags all stored here.
Build
Visual Studio or Rider. SubModule.xml manifest. Output DLL into Modules/[YourModName]/bin/Win64_Shipping_Client/.

§ XIII The Minimum Playable Build

Before building the full v1, ship an internal MVP — a feast that gathers attendees, lets the player toast, and shows an outcomes screen. Skip Phases 3-6 entirely. This validates the core loop (trigger → invite → travel → scene → outcome) and surfaces the hard problems early. Estimated MVP scope: roughly three to five weeks of solo work.

Once the MVP runs end-to-end without crashing, expand outward into Phase 3, then Phase 4, and so on. Resist the temptation to build all phases in parallel — they share architecture, and building the architecture once for Phase 2 means everything after rides on tested code.

§ XIV Implementation Phases

Listed in build order. Each phase produces a testable artefact. Time estimates are rough solo-developer days, assuming part-time pace; adjust based on your own velocity.

Step 0

Scaffolding

~3–7 days · low risk

SubModule.xml, project layout, MCM bootstrap, base CampaignBehaviorBase class registered with the campaign, empty SyncData hook, Harmony bootstrap (even if unused initially). Confirm it loads without crashing into a vanilla campaign.

Output: a mod that does nothing but appears in the launcher and does not break saves.

Step 1

Trigger system

~5–10 days · medium risk

Listen for the trigger events:

  • Settlement ownership changes (victory feast candidates)
  • Kingdom leader changes (coronation feasts)
  • Marriage events (wedding feasts)
  • War-state changes (peace celebrations)
  • Hero coming-of-age (heir feasts) — verify the event exists; may need a daily-tick check on hero ages instead

Build the per-kingdom scheduler with cooldowns. AI faction picks a host (highest-relation fief-holder to the trigger event). Player-trigger menu in the Clan screen.

Risk: exact event names need verification. Plan a half-day for decompiling CampaignEvents and finding what fires when.

Output: in-game notification "Lord X is hosting a feast at [fief]" appears at appropriate moments.

Step 2

Invitations & RSVPs

~5–7 days · low–medium risk

Generate invitation list from kingdom heroes plus diplomatic guests. Per-hero RSVP decision based on relation, distance (settlement positions are exposed), war state, rivals on the list, personality. Notification UI showing accepts/declines as they roll in.

Snub bookkeeping: not-invited allies record a relation hit applied immediately or on RSVP day.

Output: player can click a notification and see "Invited: 12. Accepted: 9. Declined: 3."

Step 3

Travel

~4–7 days · medium–high risk

Spawn or repurpose existing lord parties so they head to the host fief with an AI override that prevents normal behaviour while travelling-to-feast. Arrival detection (party reaches host settlement). Mark the lord as "at feast" — a state the rest of the system reads.

Risk: overriding lord AI behaviour is fragile and the most common source of mod incompatibility. Use the lightest-touch hook you can find. Existing diplomacy mods may show patterns.

Output: lords visibly converge on the host settlement on the campaign map over the lead-time period.

Step 4

Feast scene & Phase 1 (Mingling)

~10–15 days · high risk

The single most complex piece. Custom MissionBehavior that:

  • Loads the host's lord hall scene
  • Spawns agent-attendees for each arrived guest at appropriate positions
  • Runs a state machine for phase progression
  • Routes player click-on-agent to dialogue
  • Auto-prompts greetings on arrival animations

Phase 1's dialogue uses the existing dialogue system (XML conditions/consequences plus C# helpers). Pleasantries and topical-conversation dialogues authored here.

Risk: agent spawning into an existing scene mid-mission is the highest-risk technical task in the whole mod. Prototype it first, before any other phase work, to confirm feasibility. If it cannot be done cleanly, fall back to a stylised "feast view" UI rather than a populated 3D scene.

Output: player can enter a host's lord hall, see attendees, click them, exchange basic dialogue.

Step 5

Phase 2 (Toasts)

~10–15 days · medium risk

Composition UI (custom Gauntlet panel for the two-step picker). Audience reaction calculations (subject × style × occasion × listener preferences × Charm). Renown and relation deltas. Content task: 75–90 written toast strings. Schedule this in parallel with engineering.

Output: player can give a toast, see the room react, see deltas applied.

Step 6

Phase 7 (Conclusion) — first cut

~5–7 days · low–medium risk

Closing toast (reuses Phase 2 system with new subject list). Outcomes screen (Gauntlet UI). Persistent flag storage (memorable-feast log, "recently hosted" status, etc.). Departure handling (lords leave the settlement).

MVP Milestone

At this point, you have a complete end-to-end loop: trigger fires → invitations sent → guests travel → feast scene runs Phase 1 → Phase 2 toast → Phase 7 outcomes. Phases 3-6 are skipped. This is the MVP. Playtest aggressively before continuing. If anything is going to break, it will break here, and you want to find out before building five more phases on top.

Step 7

Phase 3 (Feasting)

~12–18 days · medium–high risk

Seating UI (drag-and-drop is heavier than a list-picker; consider list-picker for v1). Extended seated dialogue trees (personal stories, opinions, banter, romance, confession stub). Drinking-game minigame (opposed Vigor + Endurance check, three-to-five rounds, fold or push).

Risk: seating UI is custom and tedious. The dialogue category content is substantial; budget content time alongside engineering.

Step 8

Phase 4 (Tournament)

~5–10 days · medium risk

Hook into existing tournament system, populating bracket with feast guests. Archery scoring (custom — five shots, point values, two-round format). Champion's privilege flow.

Risk: depends on how moddable the tournament code is. Worst case: build a feast-specific lightweight tournament that doesn't reuse the vanilla bracket UI.

Step 9

Phase 5 (Dance & Private)

~8–12 days · medium risk

Dance abstraction (fade-to-text scene with single illustration; tone picker; relation/romance deltas). Private-conversation slot system (three slots, NPCs who seek you out are free). Romance hooks stubbed for the marriage system to consume later.

Step 10

Phase 6 (Confrontations)

~8–12 days · medium risk

Tension scoring (computed across phases, exposed via UI flags). Confrontation type roller. Type A (verbal) and Type B (duel challenge) handlers in full. Type C (brawl) simplified. Type D (revelation) as flavour stub. Duel scheduling — fire as a separate event the next dawn, reusing existing duel mechanics.

Step 11

Polish, integration, AI host behaviour

~10–20 days · medium risk, ongoing

AI host configuration (when AI lords decide to host, who they invite, how they configure feasts). Cultural flavour strings (light per-culture variation in toasts, greetings). Memorable-feast callback dialogue injection. Performance testing with thirty-guest royal feasts. Notification suppression layer during feasts (urgent events only). Bug fixing.

Part the Sixth

Open Questions

Decisions deferred from the design discussion. Each must be resolved before its corresponding implementation step.

§ XVI Architectural

Notification suppression layer — what counts as urgent during a feast?
The principle is "only war-state changes, attacks on player fiefs, and direct combat engagements interrupt." But the exact event filter list needs definition before Step 11. Routine bandit raids on distant villages, ordinary caravan losses, market fluctuations: silenced. Anything that fundamentally changes the player's strategic situation: surfaced.
NPC preference database — authored or generated?
Two approaches: (a) hand-author preferences for all named NPCs (~150-300 heroes; serious content task but precise). (b) Generate from existing personality traits (Honor → Solemn affinity, Mercy → Humble, Calculating → Witty, Valour → Boastful; fast but less expressive). Recommended: hybrid — generate baselines from traits, then hand-edit notable rulers and major characters for distinct profiles. v1 can ship generated-only; v2 adds hand-authored variations.
Standing-system formula — exact tier thresholds?
Discussed conceptually but never numerically pinned. A first-cut proposal: Commoner = no clan; Aspirant = tier 1 OR tier 0 with 100+ renown; Established = tier 2-3; Magnate = tier 4+. Cultural-match modifier: ±1 effective tier within a culture. Bloodline modifier: chargen-set, +1 effective tier even at low real tier (an exiled noble's son starts as Aspirant despite tier 0). Patronage: +1 effective tier in the patron's sphere of influence for as long as patronage holds.
Memorable-feast log capacity?
Saves can bloat. Proposed cap: 50 entries per save, expiring routine entries first (oldest non-catastrophe). Catastrophes never expire automatically. Total log size at full capacity remains under a few KB.
AI host configuration — how detailed?
The AI must decide: when to host (already covered by trigger system), guest list (default: own clan + clan tier ≥ 2 in same kingdom, ± personality-driven biases for/against rivals), scale (driven by trigger and host wealth), tournament inclusion (driven by host personality — martial lords yes, scholarly lords no). v1 should ship a deterministic-by-personality AI configuration; v2 can layer in faction-political considerations.

§ XVII Design

How does player-as-attendee experience compare to player-as-host?
The phases are identical in structure but the player's options differ in each. Phase 1: attendees greet host, hosts greet arrivals. Phase 2: attendees may be called to give a toast (high-tier) or rise voluntarily (medium-tier); hosts give the opening and closing. Phase 3: hosts arrange seating, attendees are seated. Phase 6: hosts have authority options (silence, eject, take outside), attendees have intervention options. Phase 7: hosts give closing toast, attendees do not. This needs a side-by-side spec written before Step 4.
Cross-cultural courtship modifiers — how strong?
Discussed but not weighted. Proposal: same culture = baseline; adjacent culture (e.g., Imperial-Vlandian) = relation gains/losses ×0.9; distant culture (Imperial-Khuzait) = ×0.75. Cultural alignment matters but does not block the system.
What happens if the player is a clan head with no spouse, hosting? Is there a hostess role?
In Warband, spouses played hostess. In Bannerlord, single players or widowed players exist. v1 proposal: solo hosts simply do everything themselves; family members (siblings, adult children) take on supporting roles. v2 can deepen the spouse role for partnered hosts.
Part the Seventh

Out of Scope & Future Work

The feast system is designed to be a foundation that other systems hook into, not a monolith. Several systems are out of scope for v1 but have explicit handoff points already designed:

§ XVIII Marriage System

Hooks at Phase 5 — romance climax window. The feast handles the consummation of courtship; the marriage system, when built, handles the multi-stage courtship leading up to it (introduction, discovery of preferences, poetry recitation, gift-giving, guardian's blessing, dowry negotiation) and the wedding ceremony itself (which is a configured instance of the feast system).

Scope of marriage system: a phased questline running on the campaign map, parallel to other activities, gated by the standing system. Estimated 2-3 months solo work after feasts ship.

§ XIX Conspiracy & Secrets System

Hooks at Phase 3 — confessions and secrets (private revelations of treason, debt, hidden affairs) and Phase 6 — Type D public revelations. The feast surfaces and reveals; the conspiracy system, when built, persists secrets across the campaign and creates the dramatic stakes that make Type D revelations meaningful.

§ XX Diplomacy Overhaul

Hooks at Phase 1 — strategic conversation and Phase 5 — private conversation. The feast provides the venue for diplomatic exchanges; the diplomacy system, when overhauled, defines what those exchanges can produce (alliances, trade pacts, non-aggression treaties, coalition-building against snowballers). Existing community mods (Diplomacy) cover much of this ground; integration rather than replacement is the path.

§ XXI Child-Rearing UX

Not directly hooked into feasts, but a downstream consequence of the marriage system. The vanilla "guide your child every two years" system collapses under family-focused playthroughs (hundreds of perk-allocation popups by the fourth generation). When the marriage system is built, the child-rearing UX must be rebuilt alongside it — perhaps with auto-allocate options scoped to children specifically, or a one-time per-child guidance with milestone events.

§ XXII Other Common Bannerlord Complaints — Explicitly Out of Scope

The following are widely-complained-about Bannerlord issues that this mod does not attempt to solve. Listed here so scope discipline is explicit:

  • Siege AI — separate, large mod scope. Existing fixes (Fix Siege AI on Nexus) cover this ground.
  • Battle AI pathfinding — out of scope.
  • Performance and crashes — out of scope.
  • Snowballing — touched indirectly (relation-stable kingdoms defect less, which slows the runaway-faction problem) but not directly addressed.
  • Tournament prizes / lord participation — partially addressed within feast tournaments, but vanilla tournaments untouched.
  • Co-op multiplayer — out of scope.
